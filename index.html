📊 What Is pi-telegram-bridge?

pi-telegram-bridge is a small but powerful helper for your Windows PC. It connects your coding sessions with Pi — your personal AI coding assistant — to a private Telegram bot that you control. Instead of opening a terminal, you can send instructions from your phone and receive the final result directly in your Telegram chat. It's quick, convenient, and designed with your security in mind.

This tool never gives Telegram access to your shell or command line. It only delivers the finalized answer when your Pi session is done. You stay private; your data stays on your machine. It's a local-first, practical way to work on coding tasks even when you're away from your desk.

🚦 Getting Started

1️⃣ Get Your Bot Token

Open Telegram, search for @BotFather (official bot), and start a conversation. Type /newbot and follow prompts to create your bot. You'll receive a token that looks something like this: 1234567890:ABCdefGHIJK... . Keep it safe.

2️⃣ Configure pi-telegram-bridge

Open the app after installation. You'll be asked to paste your bot token. Optionally, you can set your Telegram user ID so only you can interact with the bot. Save settings.

3️⃣ Connect to Pi

Make sure Pi (your coding agent) is installed and ready on the same PC. The bridge will automatically connect to your Pi session. No extra setup is needed.

4️⃣ Start Using It

Open your Telegram chat with your bot, send a request like "Write a function to reverse a string", and watch as pi-telegram-bridge processes it locally and returns the final code in your chat.

🛠️ How It Works Inside

pi-telegram-bridge runs as a lightweight service in the background on your Windows machine. It uses your local Node.js runtime to listen for Telegram messages, passes your prompt to your Pi session, then collects the output once your task completes. Because everything is local, there is no cloud connection, no remote access, no third-party servers processing your data.

Under the hood, the bridge leverages PowerShell to manage Pi sessions and capture the final output. It strips away all intermediate logs, showing you only what matters — the answer.
